From the Kingdom of Lo to a new world

The story of Mustang, the journey of its people, and how a community found each other again in America.

Where we come from

Ancient Mustang: the Kingdom of Lo

Mustang — the Kingdom of Lo — is a historic region in northwestern Nepal with a heritage reaching back to the ancient Tibetan and Himalayan civilizations. Once part of the Tibetan Empire, it stood as an independent kingdom until it was incorporated into Nepal in the 18th century.

Sheltered behind the Annapurna and Dhaulagiri ranges, Mustang preserved its language, monasteries, and way of life for centuries — earning its name as the “Last Forbidden Kingdom.”

Our journey

A timeline of our story

  • Ancient era

    The Kingdom of Lo flourishes

    Mustang thrives on the salt-trade route between Tibet and India. Monasteries, walled cities, and cave dwellings shape a unique Himalayan Buddhist civilization.

  • 18th century

    Union with Nepal

    The independent kingdom is incorporated into Nepal, yet keeps its royal lineage, monastic traditions, and distinct Loba identity behind the high passes.

  • Late 20th century

    The migration begins

    Seeking economic opportunity and education beyond a remote, arid homeland, Mustangi families begin to move — first to Nepal’s cities, then abroad to the United States.

  • 2003

    Mustang Kyidug USA is founded

    A group of passionate individuals in New York City establish the Kyidug to unite Mustangis in America, support newcomers, preserve culture, and give back to Mustang.

  • 2008

    The monarchy ends, the diaspora grows

    The abolition of the Mustang monarchy and political change in Nepal accelerate migration — and the Kyidug’s role as a home away from home becomes more vital than ever.

  • Today

    A pillar of the community

    Losar, Tiji, and Chhongu celebrations, support networks for new arrivals, scholarships and development projects back home — the Kyidug carries Mustang’s identity forward.

The migration

Why Mustangis came to America

Economic opportunity

Mustang is remote, high, and arid, with limited economic resources. Families sought work and stability that the region could not always provide.

Education

Higher education was scarce in Mustang. Parents sent children to study in Nepal’s cities and abroad — and many built new lives where they studied.

Political & social change

The abolition of the Mustang monarchy in 2008 and instability in Nepal encouraged families to seek certainty overseas.

Milestones

What the Kyidug has built

  • Community support networks — guidance on legal documentation, education, and job opportunities for Mustangi immigrants finding their feet in the USA.
  • Major cultural events — Losar and Chhongu festivals that reinforce cultural ties and bring hundreds of community members together each year.
  • A bridge for the young generation — programs that keep children born in America connected to the language and traditions of their homeland.
  • Giving back to Mustang — charity and development projects reaching schools, health posts, and historic sites across the region.

As Mustang Kyidug USA moves forward, our focus remains on expanding community outreach, supporting education, strengthening humanitarian efforts, and enhancing cultural documentation.
