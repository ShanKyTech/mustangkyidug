Preserving our culture, supporting our community

Mustang Kyidug USA is a nonprofit built on community, tradition, and development — a home away from home for every Mustangi in America.

Our story

Rooted in Mustang, growing in America

Mustang Kyidug USA was established to support and unite the Mustangi community living in the United States while preserving the rich cultural heritage of Mustang, Nepal. Rooted in the values of community, tradition, and development, we create a platform where Mustangi people come together, celebrate their identity, and contribute to the well-being of both their homeland and their community abroad.

Founded in 2003 in New York City by a group of passionate individuals, the Kyidug has grown into a pillar of Mustangi life in America — from festival halls in Queens to development projects in the villages of Upper Mustang.

Purpose

Our mission & vision

Mission

To preserve, promote, and protect the cultural heritage of Mustang while fostering unity and mutual support among Mustangi people living in the United States — through festivals and education, guidance and networking, and charitable projects that reach back to Mustang, Nepal.

Vision

A strong, united Mustangi community that upholds and celebrates its unique heritage while thriving in the modern world — ensuring the values, traditions, and identity of Mustang are passed on, undiminished, to future generations.

What we do

The work of the Kyidug

Cultural promotion & events

Annual celebrations for Tiji, Losar (Tibetan New Year), and Chhongu, plus community gatherings where Mustangi dance, music, and customs are showcased and taught.

Community assistance & networking

Helping new immigrants settle, sharing educational resources, and connecting professionals — a platform where Mustangi people support one another and share opportunities.

Development in Mustang

Scholarships and school improvement projects, medical aid for rural health centers, and community infrastructure — including preservation of historic sites in the Kingdom of Lo.

“We strive to be a bridge between generations — so that a child born in New York can still dance at Losar, speak to their grandmother, and know the road to Lo Manthang.” Our commitment
